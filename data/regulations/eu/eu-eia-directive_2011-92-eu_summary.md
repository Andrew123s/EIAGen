# Directive 2011/92/EU on the assessment of the effects of certain public and private projects on the environment, as amended by Directive 2014/52/EU (EIA Directive) - Curated Summary

> CURATED SUMMARY - NOT THE OFFICIAL TEXT. Only the texts published in the Official
> Journal of the European Union (EUR-Lex) are authoritative. Directives bind Member
> States and are applied to projects through national transposing legislation
> (for example the UVPG in Germany); they are not directly applicable law in
> non-EU jurisdictions such as Ghana.

## Article 2(1) - Projects subject to assessment
Member States shall adopt all measures necessary to ensure that, before development
consent is given, projects likely to have significant effects on the environment by
virtue, inter alia, of their nature, size or location are made subject to a
requirement for development consent and an assessment with regard to their effects
on the environment.
Obligation: Projects likely to have significant environmental effects require development consent and environmental assessment before approval.

## Article 3 - Factors to be assessed
The environmental impact assessment identifies, describes and assesses the direct and
indirect significant effects of a project on: population and human health;
biodiversity, with particular attention to species and habitats protected under the
Habitats and Birds Directives; land, soil, water, air and climate; material assets,
cultural heritage and the landscape; and the interaction between these factors. The
effects include those deriving from the vulnerability of the project to risks of
major accidents and/or disasters.
Obligation: Assess effects on population, human health, biodiversity, land, soil, water, air, climate, cultural heritage and landscape.

## Article 4(1) - Annex I projects
Projects listed in Annex I shall be made subject to an assessment in accordance with
Articles 5 to 10.
Obligation: Carry out an EIA for Annex I projects.
Permit: Development consent following EIA

## Article 4(2)-(4) - Annex II projects and screening
For projects listed in Annex II, Member States determine whether the project shall
be made subject to an assessment through a case-by-case examination and/or
thresholds or criteria set by the Member State, taking into account the selection
criteria in Annex III. The developer provides information on the characteristics of
the project and its likely significant effects (Annex IIA).
Obligation: Submit Annex IIA screening information for Annex II projects so the competent authority can make a screening determination.

## Annex I, point 7 - Motorways and roads
Annex I includes the construction of motorways and express roads, and the
construction of a new road of four or more lanes, or realignment and/or widening of
an existing road of two lanes or less so as to provide four or more lanes, where such
new road or realigned and/or widened section of road would be 10 km or more in a
continuous length.
Obligation: Carry out an EIA where a road project meets the Annex I point 7 description.

## Annex I, point 17 - Intensive rearing of poultry or pigs
Annex I includes installations for the intensive rearing of poultry or pigs with
more than 85 000 places for broilers, 60 000 places for hens, 3 000 places for
production pigs (over 30 kg) or 900 places for sows.
Obligation: Carry out an EIA for livestock installations exceeding the Annex I point 17 capacities.

## Annex I, point 19 - Quarries and open-cast mining
Annex I includes quarries and open-cast mining where the surface of the site exceeds
25 hectares, or peat extraction where the surface of the site exceeds 150 hectares.
Obligation: Carry out an EIA for quarries and open-cast mining with a site surface over 25 hectares.

## Annex II - Projects subject to Member State determination
Annex II covers, among others, agriculture projects (including intensive livestock
installations not in Annex I and water management for agriculture), extractive
industry not in Annex I, energy industry (including installations for the harnessing
of wind power for energy production - wind farms), production and processing of
metals and minerals, food and chemical industries, infrastructure projects
(including urban development projects and construction of roads not in Annex I),
and waste disposal installations not in Annex I.
Obligation: Obtain a screening determination from the competent authority for Annex II projects.

## Annex III - Selection criteria for screening
The criteria are the characteristics of projects (size and design, cumulation, use of
natural resources, production of waste, pollution and nuisances, risk of major
accidents, risks to human health), the location of projects (environmental
sensitivity of areas, including wetlands, riparian areas, river mouths, coastal
zones, mountain and forest areas, nature reserves and parks, Natura 2000 sites,
densely populated areas and landscapes of historical or cultural significance) and
the type and characteristics of the potential impact.
Obligation: Address Annex III criteria in screening information.

## Article 5 and Annex IV - EIA report
Where an assessment is required, the developer prepares an EIA report including a
description of the project, a description of the likely significant effects, the
measures envisaged to avoid, prevent, reduce and if possible offset significant
adverse effects, a description of the reasonable alternatives studied, a
non-technical summary and any additional information specified in Annex IV.
Obligation: Prepare an EIA report with the information required by Article 5 and Annex IV.
