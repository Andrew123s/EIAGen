# Environmental Assessment Regulations, 1999 (L.I. 1652) - Curated Summary

> CURATED SUMMARY - NOT THE OFFICIAL TEXT. Prepared for EIAGen as a retrieval aid.
> Regulation and schedule references must be checked against the official
> Legislative Instrument before any reliance. Subsequent amendments and the effect
> of later primary legislation on these Regulations have not been verified.

## Regulation 1 - Registration of new undertakings (Schedule 1)
No person shall commence an undertaking listed in Schedule 1 unless the undertaking
has been registered with the Agency and an environmental permit has been issued for
it. Schedule 1 covers a broad range of undertakings, including agriculture, mining
and quarrying, manufacturing and industrial processing, energy, transport and road
infrastructure, waste management, water supply and housing and urban development.
Obligation: Register the undertaking and obtain an environmental permit before commencement.
Permit: Environmental permit

## Regulation 3 - Undertakings requiring environmental impact assessment (Schedule 2)
An undertaking listed in Schedule 2 requires an environmental impact assessment
before an environmental permit is issued. Schedule 2 lists undertakings for which
an EIA is mandatory; inclusion depends on the type of undertaking and, for some
entries, on its scale. The scale thresholds in Schedule 2 are not reproduced in
this summary and must be read from the official text.
Obligation: Prepare an environmental impact statement where the undertaking falls within Schedule 2.
Permit: Environmental permit following EIA

## Schedule 5 - Environmentally sensitive areas
The Regulations identify environmentally sensitive areas, such as areas declared as
national parks, watershed or forest reserves, wildlife reserves and sanctuaries,
areas of cultural or historic significance, wetlands, coastal zones and areas prone
to natural hazards. Undertakings located in or near such areas, for example near a
river, wetland or protected area, warrant particular scrutiny of the need for EIA.
Obligation: Identify whether the undertaking lies in or near an environmentally sensitive area and disclose this in the application.

## Screening of applications
On registration, the Agency screens the undertaking and may decide to issue a
permit, to object, or to require a preliminary environmental report (PER) or a full
environmental impact assessment, depending on the potential significance of the
environmental effects. Screening considers the location, size and output of the
undertaking, the technology to be used, the concerns of the public, and the land
use and other factors relevant to the area.
Obligation: Provide the information required for screening in the prescribed registration form.

## Preliminary environmental report
Where required by the Agency, the applicant submits a preliminary environmental
report describing the undertaking, the likely environmental effects and the
proposed mitigation measures. Based on the report, the Agency may issue a permit or
require a full environmental impact assessment.
Obligation: Prepare a preliminary environmental report where directed.

## Scoping and terms of reference
For an undertaking requiring EIA, the applicant submits a scoping report with draft
terms of reference identifying the key issues to be assessed. The scoping notice is
publicised so that concerned parties can raise issues.
Obligation: Prepare a scoping report and draft terms of reference; publicise the scoping notice.

## Environmental impact statement
The environmental impact statement describes the undertaking and alternatives, the
existing environment (baseline), the potential positive and negative impacts during
construction, operation and decommissioning, mitigation measures, an environmental
management plan and monitoring programme, and the results of consultation.
Obligation: Prepare and submit the environmental impact statement to the Agency.

## Public hearings and consultation
The Agency may hold a public hearing where there is great public concern about the
undertaking, where the undertaking would involve dislocation or relocation of
communities, or where the undertaking could have extensive and far-reaching effects
on the environment.
Obligation: Participate in public consultation and any public hearing directed by the Agency.

## Environmental management plan and permit conditions
Holders of environmental permits are required to submit environmental management
plans within the period specified and to comply with the conditions of the permit,
including monitoring and reporting obligations. Permits are subject to renewal.
Obligation: Submit an environmental management plan and comply with permit conditions and monitoring requirements.
Permit: Environmental permit (with conditions)
